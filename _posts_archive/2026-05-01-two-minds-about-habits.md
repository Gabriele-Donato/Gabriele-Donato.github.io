<p>Two accounts of habit formation get argued past each other so often that it
is worth being explicit about what each one is actually claiming.</p>
<p>The cue-level account explains installation: a habit is a decision made
once and delegated to a trigger, so repetition frequency matters more than
the intensity of any single instance.</p>
<p>The identity-level account explains something the first one cannot: why a
habit survives the disappearance of its cue. That requires the repetitions to
have accumulated into a self-description, not just an automatism.</p>
<p>Both are correct, and about different halves of the same phenomenon.</p>
