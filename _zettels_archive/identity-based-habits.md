Behaviour change that survives the disappearance of its cue is not behaviour
change at all. It is a revised self-description, and the actions are only the
evidence that made the revision credible.

The mechanism is cheap to state and hard to fake: each repetition is a datum,
the self-concept is the posterior, and a single conspicuous exception updates
it far more than a week of quiet compliance. Which is why "I am the sort of
person who does X" is worth more than a streak counter, and why breaking a
streak is expensive out of all proportion to the lost repetition.

Note that this is the same asymmetry that shows up in `42a`: the cue-level
account explains installation, but only the identity-level account explains
persistence.
